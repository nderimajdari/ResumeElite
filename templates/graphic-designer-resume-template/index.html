Your resume is a design sample

A design lead will judge the resume itself before reading it: hierarchy, spacing, type pairing, alignment. That is why a designer's resume can be more expressive than an accountant's, but it still has to be easy to read in thirty seconds.

Link your portfolio first

The most important line on the page is your portfolio URL. Put it in the contact block on its own line, short and readable (yourname.design rather than a long platform link). The downloaded PDF keeps links clickable.

Projects before experience

List three or four projects you want to be asked about in the interview:

Rebrand for a regional coffee chain: identity, packaging and signage for 34 stores. Sales of the new seasonal range were up 18%.

Editorial illustration for a national magazine's annual report issue; shortlisted for a D&AD award.

Name the client, the scope and, when you have one, a result.

Tools and skills

Keep tools in the sidebar (Figma, Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, InDesign, After Effects) and put skills that describe judgement, such as typography, brand systems and art direction, in the main column or summary.

Photo

Optional. Many European studios expect one; in the US and UK it is your choice. The sidebar looks complete without it.
