What a head teacher reads for

Three things: what and who you have taught, whether you can run a room, and whether your pupils improved. The first is easy and most resumes stop there. The last one is what gets you an interview.

Writing teaching experience

For each school give the subject, the year groups or grades, and class sizes. Then bullets that show results:

Raised the Year 11 English pass rate from 61% to 79% in two years.

>

Designed the department's Key Stage 3 reading curriculum, now used by 6 teachers.

>

Introduced restorative behaviour routines; exclusions in my form group fell by half.

If you do not have exam data, use what you do have: reading ages, attendance, assessment growth, clubs started, parent feedback.

Certification and licensure

State licence or QTS, endorsements, and the date. Add safeguarding, first aid, SEN or ESL qualifications here too — schools filter on them.

The teaching profile

The summary is titled Teaching Profile. Two or three lines on your subject, the age range you teach best, and one thing you are known for. Avoid "passionate educator"; every applicant is.

Student teachers

Put each placement under Teaching Experience with the school, subject and weeks, and lead with your strongest lesson sequence.

If this one is not right

Three close alternatives, and every one carries your content across untouched when you switch.
