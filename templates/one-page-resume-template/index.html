When one page is required

Many employers and programmes ask for a one-page resume: consulting firms, banks, graduate schemes, career fairs and most roles in the US for candidates with under ten years of experience. A compact layout gives you room without resorting to 8-point type.

How to fit more without cramming

The layout saves space; your editing should too.

  1. Cut old roles to one line. Title, employer, years. No bullets.
  2. Two to four bullets per job, most recent jobs getting the most.
  3. One line per bullet where possible. If a bullet wraps by two words, rewrite it.
  4. Inline lists. Skills and languages on a single line each, not as columns.
  5. Drop what everyone assumes: "References available on request", a full street address, and objective statements.

How to know if it fits

The editor shows page breaks live. If your content spills to a second page, the preview shows it immediately; cut until it fits. If it still spills after honest editing, two pages are fine for most experienced candidates, and the Professional or Chronological templates use the space well.
