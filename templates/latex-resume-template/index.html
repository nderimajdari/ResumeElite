The LaTeX Style resume template

The look of a resume written in LaTeX, with none of the setup. A Times-style serif, dates hung in the left margin and quiet ruled headings, the style familiar from computer science departments and academic CVs. You type into a form; there is no code, no compiler and no Overleaf project.

Why people want the LaTeX look

In technical fields the LaTeX resume has become a signal of its own: clean typography, dense but readable, no decoration. Hiring managers in software, research and finance see hundreds of them and read them quickly.

Getting that look usually means learning a document class, fixing compile errors and fighting with spacing. This template gives you the same typographic result in a regular editor, with a live preview.

Filling it in for a technical role

Education first if you are a student or recent graduate: university, degree, expected graduation, GPA if it is strong, and relevant coursework.

Experience and projects with one-line bullets that lead with a verb and include a number or a technology:

Cut p95 API latency from 480 ms to 120 ms by adding a Redis read-through cache.

Built a CUDA kernel for sparse attention; 3.1x faster than the PyTorch baseline.

Skills as plain lists: languages, frameworks, tools. Skip rating bars; they mean nothing to the reader.

For academic CVs

Add Publications, Teaching, Grants and Conferences as sections. The margin date column keeps a long list of entries easy to scan. For a full research CV, the Academic CV template has hanging-indent publication entries.
