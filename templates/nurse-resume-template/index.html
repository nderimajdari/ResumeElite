What a nurse manager checks first

Licence, then specialty, then unit. Before anyone reads a bullet they want to know that you are licensed where the job is, that your certifications are current, and whether you have worked a unit like theirs. The side rail puts all three in one place: Licenses & Certifications sits directly under your contact details.

List each one with its issuer and, where it matters, the expiry:

Registered Nurse      State Board of Nursing, #RN123456
BLS / ACLS            American Heart Association, exp. 2027
PALS / TNCC           where relevant to the unit

Writing clinical experience

Every entry needs the same four facts: unit type, bed count, ratio, and acuity. "ICU, 32 beds, 1:2, ventilated and post-surgical patients" tells a manager more than a paragraph of duties.

Then two to four bullets about outcomes, not tasks. Everyone on a med-surg floor administers medication; not everyone cut falls on their unit.

Precepted 9 new graduate nurses; all passed orientation on schedule.

>

Led a CLABSI bundle audit that took central-line infections to zero for 14 months.

New graduates

Put clinical placements in Experience with the hospital, unit and hours, and list your NCLEX status in the rail ("NCLEX-RN passed, June 2026" or "scheduled"). Your capstone placement is your strongest material — describe it like a job.

Photo or no photo

Leave it off for the US, UK, Canada and Australia; the header rebalances on its own. Much of Europe and the Middle East still expect one.
