What to put on a student resume

You have more than you think. Anything that shows you can show up, learn and finish things counts:

  • Education: school, programme, expected graduation, grades if they help.
  • Courses: three to six that relate to the job.
  • Projects: class projects, a science fair, a website, a club event you ran.
  • Experience: part-time jobs, babysitting, tutoring, volunteering, internships.
  • Skills and languages: software you use, languages you speak.

Make every line specific

The difference between a weak and a strong student resume is detail:

Weak: Member of the debate club.

Strong: Captain of the debate team; led 12 members to the regional semi-final.

Weak: Worked at a café.

Strong: Served 150+ customers a shift at a busy café; trusted to open and close the store alone after three months.

High school vs college

High school students can include GPA, honours, school clubs, sports and volunteering. College students should drop high school once they have a semester or two of university, and lead with major, relevant courses and internships.

Keep it to one page

One page is the rule for students. If it does not fit, cut older or less relevant items before you shrink the font.
