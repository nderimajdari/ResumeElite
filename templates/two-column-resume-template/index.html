The Two Column resume template

The classic two-column resume done properly. A bold nameplate with an optional photo, then experience, education and projects in a wide main column, with skills, language levels and certifications in a narrower column beside them.

  • Best forMid-career professionals with lots of skills to show
  • StructureWide main column plus a side column
  • LengthOne page, which is the point of two columns
  • ATSReal text; for strict portals pick a one-column template

Why use two columns

Short items such as skills, languages, tools and certifications waste space in a single column, each taking a full line. A side column stacks them neatly, which usually saves a third of a page and lets a strong career fit on one page.

What goes where

Main column: things a recruiter reads in full, such as summary, work experience, education and projects.

Side column: things a recruiter scans, such as skills, languages with their level, certifications and short lists like tools or interests.

Two columns and applicant tracking systems

Modern parsers handle two-column PDFs far better than they used to, and this template exports real, selectable text. Some older systems still read across both columns line by line. If you are applying through a large corporate portal and want no risk at all, switch to Simple or ATS Friendly before downloading. Your content moves with you, so it takes one click.

Keep the side column short

If the side column runs longer than the main column, move something such as certifications into the main column, or cut skills down to the ones in the job posting.
