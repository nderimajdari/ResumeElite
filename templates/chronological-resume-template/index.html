The Chronological resume template

The format most recruiters expect: your most recent job first, then back in time. Dates sit in their own left column and a dotted connector joins each entry, so progression is visible before anyone reads a word.

  • Best forSteady career progression in one field
  • StructureSingle column, date column on the left, timeline dots
  • LengthOne page; two after about ten years
  • ATSReal text in reading order; standard headings

Why recruiters like chronological resumes

They answer the recruiter's first three questions immediately: what are you doing now, how long have you done it, and is your career moving upward. The left-hand date column in this template makes the third one visible at a glance: promotions read as a staircase.

Writing each entry

Title, employer, city and dates on the first lines. Then two to five bullets, most impressive first:

Promoted from analyst to senior analyst in 18 months, ahead of the usual three years.

Rebuilt the monthly reporting pack; preparation time fell from four days to one.

Give recent jobs more bullets than older ones. Anything more than ten to fifteen years back can shrink to a single line, or go.

Dates

Use month and year ("Mar 2021 - Present") for recent roles. Years alone are fine for jobs over five years ago. If you held two roles at one employer, list the employer once and both titles under it with their own dates; it shows the promotion.

When chronological is not the right choice

If your last few titles do not match the job you want, or there is a long gap at the top of the page, look at the Functional template. If you have no work history yet, Entry Level and Student put education first.
