Resume action verbs that actually change the sentence

A stronger verb only helps if the rest of the sentence carries a result. Here is the list, and the structure it goes into.

Verb lists are the most-shared resume advice and among the least useful, because swapping one word rarely fixes a bullet. "Spearheaded the onboarding project" is not better than "worked on the onboarding project" — it is the same non-information in a louder register.

The verb matters once the sentence has something in it. So: the structure first, then the list.

The structure

Verb, object, scope, result.

Rebuilt the first-run onboarding flow for 40k monthly signups, cutting drop-off 34%.

  • Rebuilt — the verb, and specific about what kind of work it was
  • the first-run onboarding flow — the object
  • for 40k monthly signups — scope, which gives the reader scale
  • cutting drop-off 34% — the result

Drop any element and it weakens. Drop the result and you have a job description.

An even stronger variant leads with the outcome, because the first words carry most:

Cut onboarding drop-off 34% by rebuilding the first-run flow for 40k monthly signups.

Use that when the number is genuinely good. Use verb-first when it is not.

Verbs grouped by what they signal

Pick from the group that matches what you actually did. That is the whole method — these are not synonyms for each other.

You built or created something

built, designed, developed, engineered, created, launched, shipped, prototyped, architected, authored, established, founded, introduced, produced, assembled, implemented, deployed, released, published, drafted, formulated, initiated

You improved something that existed

improved, streamlined, optimised, refined, redesigned, rebuilt, upgraded, modernised, simplified, consolidated, restructured, reworked, accelerated, tightened, standardised, automated, refactored, migrated, replaced, reorganised

You reduced or removed something

reduced, cut, eliminated, decreased, lowered, minimised, halved, resolved, removed, deprecated, retired, prevented, contained, mitigated, offset

You increased or grew something

grew, increased, raised, doubled, tripled, expanded, scaled, boosted, extended, strengthened, accelerated, generated, drove, lifted, multiplied

You led people

led, managed, directed, supervised, coached, mentored, trained, hired, onboarded, built (a team), developed (people), delegated, chaired, headed, coordinated, motivated, retained, promoted

You owned a process or programme

owned, ran, oversaw, administered, maintained, operated, governed, scheduled, budgeted, forecast, planned, prioritised, sequenced, tracked, monitored, controlled

You worked across boundaries

partnered, collaborated, aligned, negotiated, brokered, liaised, consulted, briefed, influenced, persuaded, mediated, unblocked, escalated, facilitated

You analysed or investigated

analysed, evaluated, assessed, investigated, audited, diagnosed, measured, modelled, forecast, quantified, benchmarked, tested, validated, researched, surveyed, mapped, segmented, reconciled

You sold or won something

won, closed, secured, sourced, acquired, retained, upsold, renewed, pitched, converted, prospected, negotiated, signed, landed

You taught or communicated

trained, taught, presented, briefed, documented, explained, published, demonstrated, onboarded, wrote, edited, translated, facilitated

You fixed or rescued something

resolved, repaired, recovered, salvaged, turned around, stabilised, remediated, debugged, restored, corrected, unblocked

The verbs to stop using

"Responsible for" is not a verb and it removes you from your own sentence. Compare:

Responsible for the monthly close process.

Ran the monthly close for 4 entities, cutting the cycle from 9 days to 5.

"Helped" and "assisted with" volunteer that you were not the one who did it. If you genuinely contributed to someone else's project, name your part: "Wrote the migration scripts for a platform move led by the infrastructure team."

"Utilised" is "used" with extra syllables. Use "used", or better, cut it — the tool usually belongs at the end of the sentence, not in the verb.

"Spearheaded", "orchestrated" and "championed" are load-bearing only when the sentence has nothing in it. Led, ran and built say the same thing without announcing themselves.

Tense and consistency

Present tense for the role you are in now, past tense for everything else. Keep it consistent inside each entry — switching between "manage" and "managed" inside one job is the error people notice most.

Do not repeat the same verb

Three bullets starting with "Managed" flattens three different pieces of work into one shape. If you have genuinely managed three things, vary what you say about them:

Ran the annual planning cycle for 4 business units. Owned a GBP 40m cost centre with a 2% variance to budget. Rebuilt the forecasting model, cutting the close from 9 days to 5.

Same job, three different kinds of work now visible.

Where the numbers come from

Every verb above wants a number after it. If you think you have none:

  • Volume — tickets, users, accounts, transactions, patients, articles, covers
  • Money — budget, revenue, savings, contract value
  • Time — how long before, how long after
  • People — team size, trained, stakeholders
  • Change — any percentage, either direction

Confidential? Give the shape: "a seven-figure budget", "a team of eleven", "roughly a third". Relative change is almost never sensitive.

A quick pass over your existing resume

Ten minutes, in this order:

  1. Delete every "Responsible for".
  2. Delete every "helped" and "assisted with" — rewrite to say what you did.
  3. Find bullets with no number and add one, or cut them.
  4. Find a verb used more than twice and change two of them.
  5. Move the strongest number in each entry into the first bullet.

That pass improves most resumes more than changing the template does.
